## Part 3: why the first RF GPS fix was slow

**Short answer.** The slowness is a property of the **GPS receiver's state after it loses power**, not of the cable, the HackRF, the IQ files, the scenario, or the firmware. After every power-up the PX1120S runs in a state ("A") in which its UART is at 88.4 kbaud (115,200 x 0.767; Witness measures 88,398 to 88,403 baud) and in which it does not get a position fix: it either locks onto nothing or reads the time only. At a random moment 2.5 to 9 minutes after RF starts (in 3 of 11 watched power-ups: not within 520 to 1,000 s) it **restarts itself** (a burst of garbage, then 3 s of silence, then NMEA at 115.2 kbaud with the receiver clock back at 1980: the "UART burst, 3 s silence" seen in the slow runs). After that restart ("B") the same coax path, HackRF and IQ file give a fix in 9 to 35 s every time but once. The HITL tool puts the receiver back into state A at the end of every run: it power-cycles the Pi USB hub after the log export (`fetch_restart_hub`), and again when no fix comes in the lead-in (`power_cycle_retry`). That is why today's runs, which all started right after a hub cycle, were slow, and why 11-18 only became fast after the receiver had restarted itself once and then ran for hours (Witness uptime 6 h in `20261118-214918`) without another power cycle.

### Conclusion

1. **Cause:** receiver power-up state A of the PX1120S (UART 88.4 kbaud, no position fix, random self-restart after minutes). It is re-created by every loss of the receiver's supply, and the HITL tool causes one after every log export and after a no-fix lead-in. The owner is right that the physical setup did not change: the signal path is fine (state B acquires in 9 to 35 s on it).
2. **Fast acquisition shown** (a run with it): flight 1 (14.3 s), flight 4 (8.8 s), flight 5 (8.9 s), tests 5, 8, 10 (33.2, 35.1, 8.7 s), all with the receiver in state B; the 11-18 IQ file gives 33.2 s today against 33.4 s on 11-18.
3. **What stays unexplained:** why the PX1120S powers up in state A and what makes it restart itself (and with what delay); why one start in state B (test 4) gave no lock in 100 s. The only electrical suspect on this board is the 3.3 V rail: the Witness v0.2 regulator is fitted with VIN/VOUT swapped (about 4.3 V on the rail that powers the receiver) and the replacement is not on this board; an odd power-up state at an over-voltage rail is plausible but I did not measure it. Worth repeating the power-up test after the regulator is replaced.

### Recommendations (nothing was changed; for the owner to decide)

* **HITL tool:** do not hub-cycle as the remedy for a missing fix (`power_cycle_retry`): it puts the receiver back into the slow state. After a log export, which needs the hub cycle, expect state A: either start the RF stream early and keep a lead-in of 10 minutes or more (a restart came at 148 to 509 s of RF, but not within 520 to 1,000 s in 3 of 11 power-ups), or run a conditioning stream until the first fix (what I did: `cold_start` on, no fix after about 520 s means "hub cycle again, wait 4 minutes, retry"), then fly with a short lead-in. Use `cold_start` (default in the tool) for the state B case. The `tx` option `fetch_restart_hub` cannot simply be turned off: the exported Witness (mass-storage mode) has no serial port, so only a hub cycle returns it to flight.
* **Firmware (Witness):** nothing to fix in the GPS code; but a log line when the receiver is found at 88.4 kbaud at boot ("receiver in slow power-up state") would make this visible in every log, and a GPS reset/enable line from the MCU on the next board revision would let Witness do what the receiver does on its own after minutes (restart it) at boot.
* **Hardware:** re-test the power-up behaviour on the corrected 3.3 V rail.

## Other open findings

1. **Flight without any GPS (flight 2, `20261119-152146`).** The receiver never locked during the whole 480 s lead-in and the 430 s flight (a state-A power-up without a restart in 900 s of RF), so no fix at all. The filter ran on IMU and barometer only: up error max 3,471 m, RMS 1,309 m, 21.6 % of height at 190 s in Descent, 20.6 % of the time over 5 %; Descent was detected 2.28 s early (111.68 s against 113.96 s, still inside the 10 s tolerance); all other transitions +0.02 s. Same family as finding 2 of the earlier 11-19 report (a flight without a fix), with no mid-flight anchoring because GPS never came. The log is complete (session `12F`).
2. **HITL tool continues silently with no fix** (flight 2: "no GPS fix during the 480 s lead-in; the run continues but GPS will join late"). It then flies a whole 15 minute run that is useless as a GPS test. A check "no fix: stop before ENTER" with the power-cycle retry off would save the run.
3. **Radio loss in Descent is still there, but small:** 0 to 5 one-second gaps (flights 1, 3, 4, 5, 6: 0, 4, 0, 5, 0 packets; reference 2). Not different from before.
4. **`Coast` in flight 4 was detected from the status packet (+0.07 s), not from the log record,** the other detections are from the log. All inside the tolerance; not investigated.
5. **Bench scripts.** `flashit.ps1` for Aperture aborts at the esptool "deprecated" warning because the script sets `$ErrorActionPreference = 'Stop'` and the warning goes to stderr; the same command works run directly. `flash_verified.ps1` printed the esptool warning in red but finished normally.
6. **I touched Iris's flash port read-only** (an app-descriptor read to tell the boards apart). It kept running afterwards; the `--after no-reset` form can leave a board in the bootloader, so the next time use Witness's `flash_verified.ps1 -CheckOnly` style (which ends with a hard reset) or `bench_ports` only.
7. **The printer** on the same table was not noted to run; IMU-related numbers (attitude 0.93 to 0.97 deg final tilt error) are normal, so I did not look for vibration effects.
